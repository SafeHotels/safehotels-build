Durham rarely appears on any list of the UK's higher-crime cities, and visitors tend to describe it as one of England's calmest and most relaxed city breaks — a UNESCO World Heritage cathedral and castle on a wooded peninsula, small enough to cross on foot in fifteen minutes. That reputation holds up under the data, though it isn't quite the whole picture.

Durham is a small city that carries an outsized share of County Durham's retail and nightlife activity, plus a large student population that swells well beyond the official headcount during term time. That combination shows up clearly in the crime figures, even though the risk to an aware visitor staying in the right area remains low. This guide uses real Police.uk data — the same source behind our live safety scores — to show you exactly where to base yourself and where to keep your wits about you.

Durham Safety Overview

Durham is policed by Durham Constabulary, and Durham City records around 85 crimes per 1,000 people in the most recent 12 months of Police.uk data — about 18% above the England and Wales average, though actually below the wider County Durham rate of roughly 93 per 1,000. In other words, the city that gives the county its name isn't what's pushing the county's figures upward.

The breakdown matters more than the headline number. Shoplifting is Durham's biggest outlier, running at more than two and a half times the national rate, concentrated almost entirely in the Milburngate and Prince Bishops shopping centres along North Road rather than in residential streets or around hotels. Violence and sexual offences are the single largest category by volume, but the rate itself sits only marginally above the national average. For visitors, the more useful numbers are the low ones: theft from the person and vehicle crime both run at well under half the national rate, meaning opportunistic street theft aimed at tourists is genuinely uncommon.

Is Durham Safe for Solo Female Travellers?

Yes. Durham's combination of a small, compact centre, heavy footfall around the university estate, and very low theft-from-person figures make it one of the more reassuring English cities for a woman travelling alone. The Bailey and Neville's Cross both carry our Solo Female Friendly badge, reflecting consistently low night-time incident rates in the crime data for those postcodes.

The honest counterweight is the one that applies to most UK university cities: the New Elvet and Walkergate bar strip gets busy and boisterous on weekend nights, particularly during term time and graduation week in the summer. It's a high-footfall, well-policed area rather than a genuinely risky one, but a quieter base a short walk away in Old Elvet or the Bailey keeps you clear of the loudest end of the evening.

Solo Female Traveller Note: The Bailey is our top pick for women travelling alone in Durham. Much of the peninsula sits within Durham University's college grounds, which means restricted vehicle access and a genuinely quiet, watched-over feel after dark that's hard to find elsewhere in a city centre this central.

Areas to Approach with Caution

The Wharton Park footpath from the railway station is the one route we'd actively steer travellers away from after dark — it's unlit, quiet, and sits at the top of Durham's anti-social behaviour figures. Take the road route down North Road instead, or a taxi if you're arriving late in the evening. North Road and the Milburngate/Prince Bishops shopping core record the city's shoplifting hotspot; it's a daytime retail-crime story rather than a threat to visitors, but it's worth keeping bags zipped while you're browsing. Beyond the centre, Gilesgate is a largely residential area with little to offer a visitor — there are simply better, more central options at comparable prices.

Practical Safety Tips for Durham

🚶
Stay Inside the Loop

Durham's historic core sits inside a single bend of the River Wear, and almost everything you'll want — the Cathedral, Castle, Bailey, Elvet and the Market Place — is within a 10-minute walk of it. Staying inside or right on the loop keeps your evening walks short and well populated.

📍
Check the Postcode First

Enter any hotel's postcode into our search tool to see live crime data for that exact area. DH1 3 (the Bailey and Old Elvet side) is the sweet spot for safe, central stays.

🌙
Skip the Station Footpath After Dark

Arriving by train in the evening, take North Road rather than the Wharton Park footpath down into the city — it's better lit and far busier.

🎓
Watch the University Calendar

Graduation week and the Durham Miners' Gala both fall in July and bring large crowds, with a livelier Elvet and Walkergate in the evenings. Book hotels early and expect busier nights around those dates.

When to Visit Durham

Spring and early summer (April–June) are the easiest months for a solo visit — Durham University is still in term, giving the city a lived-in, busy-but-safe feel, and long evenings mean you're rarely walking in the dark. July brings the Durham Miners' Gala and graduation ceremonies, both wonderful to see but genuinely crowded — book well ahead and expect the Peninsula and Elvet to be livelier than usual, in a good way.

Late autumn and winter are quieter and cheaper, with the Cathedral's Christmas events adding some evening buzz to an otherwise calm off-season city. The trade-off is more of your day happening after dark, so lean on the well-lit central routes around the Bailey and Old Elvet rather than the Wharton Park approach.
