Gatwick is the UK's second busiest airport, and a lot of its passengers face the same question: where do you sleep before a 6am departure, or after a landing close to midnight? The airport sits on the border of two counties. The terminals themselves fall within Crawley borough in West Sussex and are policed by Sussex Police, while Horley, the town immediately to the north, is in Surrey and covered by Surrey Police. That split matters, because the crime picture changes noticeably depending on which side of the runway you stay.

The good news is that the safest options are also some of the most convenient. A handful of hotels connect directly to the terminals by walkway, and the towns to the north record crime well below the national average. This guide uses real Police.uk data, the same source behind our live safety scores, to show exactly where to book and which areas are better passed through than slept in.

Is it safe to stay near Gatwick Airport?

Yes, for the vast majority of travellers. The terminal hotels sit inside one of the most heavily staffed and monitored environments in the South East, and the residential towns of Horley and Reigate rank comfortably among the safer places in Surrey. The variation shows up to the south, where Crawley town centre records crime above the national average, and along the airport perimeter, where budget hotels sit on busy main roads and you rely on shuttle buses after dark.

It also helps to know why some airport figures look alarming on paper. Very few people live in the airport postcodes, yet millions pass through them, so any crime recorded inside the terminals produces a high rate per head. That is a real number, but it says more about the terminal concourse than about the corridor between your room and your gate.

What do the Police.uk crime figures actually say?

In the latest Police.uk data (12 months to April 2026), Crawley borough records around 90 crimes per 1,000 people, roughly 26% above the national average and about 51% above the West Sussex rate of 60. Crawley's figures include the airport itself, which adds a large volume of recorded crime to a modest residential population.

Cross to the north of the airport and the picture changes. Horley records around 51 crimes per 1,000 people, about 30% below the national average and slightly below the Surrey-wide rate. Reigate, roughly 20 minutes away by taxi, is lower again at around 38 per 1,000, almost half the national rate.

Where are the safe areas on a map?

Get your bearings before you book. The terminal hotels sit right on the airport, Horley lies immediately to the north with Reigate further up the A217, Copthorne sits to the east by the M23, and Crawley town centre is to the south.

Are the terminal-connected hotels worth the extra cost?

For an early flight or a late arrival, usually yes. Gatwick has three hotels linked to the terminals by walkway: the Sofitel and the Hampton by Hilton at the North Terminal, and the Hilton at the South Terminal. A few others, such as the Premier Inn at the North Terminal and the Courtyard by Marriott near the South Terminal, are on airport land but involve a short outdoor walk or a shuttle.

The difference is mostly about exposure. With a connected hotel, the only time you are outdoors is when you choose to be, which removes the dark car park, the shuttle stop and the taxi rank from your night. If your flight leaves before 7am or lands after 11pm, the premium over a perimeter hotel is often worth paying for that alone. Check which terminal your airline uses first, because the free shuttle train between North and South adds a few minutes to the journey.

Is Gatwick safe for solo female travellers?

Yes. For women travelling alone, the strongest choices are the terminal-connected hotels for convenience and the residential side of Horley and Reigate for a calmer evening. Horley and Reigate both carry our Solo Female Friendly badge, which reflects low night-time incident rates in the Police.uk data for those postcodes.

If you are choosing between a cheaper perimeter hotel and a connected one for a solo late arrival, we would lean towards the connected option. The perimeter hotels are not unsafe, but waiting alone at a shuttle stop on a main road at midnight is the part of the trip most worth designing out when you are travelling on your own.

Solo Female Traveller Note: For a late landing, book a terminal-connected hotel. For a relaxed night before a flight, Reigate offers the lowest crime of any town near Gatwick, while Horley gives you low crime and a one-stop train to the terminal.

Is it safe to arrive at Gatwick late at night?

The terminals themselves stay staffed and well lit through the night, with police and security on site. The quieter moments come after you leave them. The routes to off-site car parks, the shuttle stops along the perimeter roads and the walk from Crawley or Horley stations late at night all see far fewer people than the daytime crowds suggest.

Our advice is practical rather than alarming. If you land late, either stay connected to the terminal or pre-book a taxi from the official rank, and avoid walking along the perimeter roads to save a shuttle fare. If you are using a hotel shuttle, check its last departure time before you fly, because a missed shuttle at 1am is what turns a routine arrival into an awkward one.

Which areas should I be cautious about?

Crawley town centre, around the High Street and Queens Square, records the highest street-level crime of the areas most visitors consider, led by violence and anti-social behaviour and busiest late on Friday and Saturday nights. The perimeter roads around Povey Cross and Hookwood are fine for a hotel stay but not for walking after dark. Off-site and long-stay car parks are where vehicle crime concentrates, so that is the place to think about your car rather than yourself. Beyond these specific spots, there is nowhere around Gatwick we would tell a traveller to avoid during the day.

When to book a Gatwick hotel

Gatwick hotels fill up fastest during summer, school holidays and bank holiday weekends, when the connected hotels sell out early and prices climb across the board. Midweek nights in autumn and winter are the sweet spot, with cheaper rates and quieter hotels and no change to the safety picture. Whatever the season, book a connected room well ahead if your flight leaves before 7am, because those are the rooms that go first.

Shorter winter days also change the calculation. If you are staying in Horley, Copthorne or on the perimeter, more of your travel to and from the airport will happen in the dark between November and February, which is another reason to favour the terminal hotels for very early or very late flights in those months.
